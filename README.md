Forward and inverse problems in the characterization of self-healing process of asphalt mixture using the air-coupled nonlinear ultrasonic technique

Abstract：
Conventional linear ultrasonic techniques lack sensitivity for detecting microdamage, whereas nonlinear ultrasonic, though more sensitive, remains complex and inefficient for rapid pavement assessment. These practical limitations give rise to two primary challenges in characterizing the self-healing process of asphalt mixture via air-coupled nonlinear ultrasonic technique: (1) accurately predicting ultrasonic signals from material damage parameters (forward problem), and (2) determining critical mechanical parameters from single ultrasonic signals (inverse problem). To address the forward problem, a deep learning framework integrating Convolutional Autoencoder with Physics-Informed Deep Learning (CAE-PIDL) is proposed. The CAE-PIDL model captures time-domain information through convolutional layers while incorporating frequency-domain physical information loss during training. This integration achieves a 7.3-fold reduction in training loss and a 4.8-fold reduction in validation loss for frequency-domain features compared to the traditional Mean Squared Error-based model (CAE-MSE), demonstrating superior generalization and robustness. For the ill-posed inverse problem, a CAE-Multilevel Discrete Wavelet Decomposition (CAE-MDWT) model accurately determines multiple mechanical parameters from a single ultrasonic signal by leveraging spatial and temporal information. The high prediction accuracy is attributed to the crucial roles of fundamental and harmonic components in ultrasonic signals. The inclusion of these components significantly enhances the precision and reliability of self-healing process assessments. The results demonstrate that the proposed deep learning frameworks substantially enhance the efficiency and accuracy of ultrasonic testing for asphalt mixture self-healing evaluation
